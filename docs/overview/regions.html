Analysis Regions

The Hudson Bay Sea Ice Analysis workflow computes daily sea ice statistics for five predefined regions covering the complete Hudson Bay system.

Overview

Rather than analysing the Arctic Ocean as a whole, this project focuses on the Hudson Bay system. Five configurable polygon regions allow different parts of the system to be analysed independently while maintaining a common processing workflow.

Each region exhibits its own seasonal freeze-up and break-up characteristics. The subdivision therefore provides considerably more insight than analysing only a single aggregated sea ice extent.

Individual Regions

Hudson Bay Area

The Hudson Bay Area represents the complete study area and includes all individual subregions analysed by the workflow. It provides an overall indicator of the seasonal evolution of sea ice throughout the Hudson Bay system.

Purpose

Complete analysis domain

Contains

All subregions

Hudson Bay

Hudson Bay forms the central basin of the study area and experiences a pronounced annual freeze-up and melt cycle. It represents the largest individual water body analysed within the project.

Characteristics

Largest basin

Focus

Seasonal ice evolution

Foxe Basin

Foxe Basin is located north of Hudson Bay and generally exhibits a longer ice season than the central basin. The region provides an important comparison for analysing regional differences in sea ice persistence.

Characteristics

Long ice season

Focus

Northern comparison region

Gulf of Boothia

The Gulf of Boothia connects the northern Hudson Bay system with the Canadian Arctic Archipelago. Persistent sea ice conditions make this region particularly interesting for long-term observations.

Characteristics

High ice persistence

Focus

Northern gateway

Hudson Strait

Hudson Strait forms the connection between Hudson Bay and the Labrador Sea. Its dynamic ice conditions strongly influence the exchange between the interior basin and the North Atlantic.

Region Definitions

All analysis regions are defined by configurable GeoJSON polygons. During processing these polygons are converted into raster masks that are subsequently used for the daily computation of regional sea ice statistics.

Separating the region definitions from the processing code makes it easy to modify existing regions or introduce additional study areas without changing the underlying analysis workflow.
