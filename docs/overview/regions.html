Analysis Regions

The Hudson Bay Sea Ice Analysis workflow computes daily sea ice statistics for five predefined regions covering the complete Hudson Bay system.

Overview

Rather than analysing the Arctic Ocean as a whole, this project focuses on the Hudson Bay system. Five configurable polygon regions allow different parts of the system to be analysed independently while maintaining a common processing workflow.

Each region exhibits its own seasonal freeze-up and break-up characteristics. The subdivision therefore provides considerably more insight than analysing only a single aggregated sea ice extent.

Hudson Bay

Hudson Bay forms the central basin of the study area and is by far the largest individual region analysed within the workflow. Owing to its large surface area, it dominates the seasonal evolution of the entire Hudson Bay system while still exhibiting substantial interannual variability in both freeze-up and break-up.

Scientific Relevance

Because Hudson Bay contains the largest fraction of the total water area, its seasonal sea-ice cycle is often used as an overall indicator of regional climate variability. Long-term observations reveal considerable changes in both the duration and intensity of the annual ice season.

Foxe Basin

Foxe Basin is located north of Hudson Bay and is characterised by one of the longest sea-ice seasons within the study area. Owing to its northern location, freeze-up generally begins earlier and break-up occurs later than in the central Hudson Bay, making the basin particularly valuable for monitoring regional climate variability.

Scientific Relevance

Foxe Basin generally retains sea ice considerably longer than Hudson Bay and therefore serves as an important northern reference region when comparing seasonal ice persistence throughout the Hudson Bay system.

Gulf of Boothia

The Gulf of Boothia forms the northernmost study region and connects Hudson Bay with the Canadian Arctic Archipelago. It experiences persistent sea-ice conditions throughout much of the year and often remains ice covered well after southern regions have become ice free.

Scientific Relevance

Persistent sea ice and the northern location make the Gulf of Boothia an excellent indicator of changes affecting the Canadian Arctic and the northern Hudson Bay system.

Hudson Strait

Hudson Strait forms the only marine connection between Hudson Bay and the Labrador Sea. Sea-ice conditions are strongly influenced by ocean exchange with the North Atlantic, resulting in considerably more dynamic seasonal behaviour than in the enclosed Hudson Bay basin.

Scientific Relevance

Hudson Strait represents the primary exchange pathway between Hudson Bay and the North Atlantic. Variations in sea-ice conditions therefore influence the transport of heat, freshwater and sea ice between both systems.

Regional Characteristics

Although all five regions belong to the Hudson Bay system, their seasonal sea-ice evolution differs considerably. Northern regions generally remain ice covered for substantially longer periods than the southern basin, while Hudson Strait exhibits the strongest influence of the North Atlantic.

Analysing the regions separately therefore provides much more information than considering only the total sea-ice extent of the complete study area.

Region Definitions

All analysis regions are defined by configurable GeoJSON polygons. During processing these polygons are converted into raster masks that are subsequently used for the daily computation of regional sea ice statistics.

Separating the region definitions from the processing code makes it easy to modify existing regions or introduce additional study areas without changing the underlying analysis workflow.
